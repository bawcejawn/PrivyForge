Extract text from a PDF, scanned pages included

Copy the exact text out of digital PDFs and read scanned pages with OCR, all inside your browser, so the document never leaves your device.

Drop a PDF here or click to choose One file at a time · text pages are instant · scanned pages use OCR (English)

    If your PDF has scanned pages, first use downloads about 7 MB of OCR data to your browser; it's cached for next time. PDFs with real text don't need it. The download comes from this site; your PDF stays on your device.

      How to get text out of a PDF

      1. Add your PDF by dropping it on the box or clicking to choose it.
      2. Choose pages (optional). Leave the box empty for the whole document, or type ranges such as 1-3, 7 or 5- (page 5 to the end).
      3. Click "Extract text". Each page is checked for real text first. Pages without it are read with OCR, which takes longer; a progress bar shows how far along it is.
      4. Review the result. The text appears in an editable box, with a line such as --- Page 2 (OCR) --- before each page. Small labels above the box show which pages came from the PDF's own text and which were OCR'd.
      5. Copy or download. "Download .txt" saves a plain-text (UTF-8) file named after your PDF.

      Two kinds of PDF page

      A PDF exported from Word, Google Docs, a web browser or accounting software stores its text as real characters. For those pages, this tool uses pdf.js to read the characters directly. That is exact (no guessing), fast, and works for any language the PDF contains. The pieces of text are put back into reading order: lines from top to bottom, words from left to right, and a blank line where the gap between lines suggests a new paragraph.

      A scanned PDF is different: each page is just a picture of paper, with no characters inside. When a page has fewer than 20 letters or digits in its text layer (so a lone page number doesn't count), or its "text" is mostly unreadable symbols, the page is drawn at 300 DPI and read with Tesseract.js OCR. At 300 DPI a US Letter page becomes a 2,550 × 3,300 pixel image, which is the resolution OCR engines are usually tuned for. These pages are marked "(OCR)" in the output.

      Tick OCR all pages when a PDF has a text layer you don't trust, for example a scan that was run through a poor OCR program before, or when the extracted text comes out jumbled. The page is then read from its image instead.

      Worked example

      Say you drop in a 12-page contract where pages 1–10 were typed and pages 11–12 are signed pages that were scanned back in. Pages 1–10 come out as exact text in a moment. Pages 11 and 12 have no text layer, so they are rendered and read with OCR; the first time you do this, the OCR engine and English data download first (about 7 MB, once). Your output file contains twelve sections, the last two headed --- Page 11 (OCR) --- and --- Page 12 (OCR) ---, so you know exactly which parts to proofread. The signatures themselves won't turn into names: OCR reads printed text, not handwriting.

      Limits worth knowing

      • OCR accuracy depends on scan quality. Clean, straight, high-contrast scans read well. Faxes, photocopies of photocopies, skewed pages and coloured backgrounds produce errors.
      • OCR is English only for now. Digital text in any language extracts fine; scanned pages in other languages won't be read correctly.
      • Handwriting is read poorly. Expect gibberish for cursive and signatures.
      • Layout is not kept. You get plain text. Multi-column pages and tables can come out in a different order than you read them.
      • Password-protected PDFs need to be unlocked in your PDF reader first.
      • Speed. Text pages take a fraction of a second. OCR takes several seconds per page, more on older phones.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is my PDF uploaded?

      No. The PDF is opened and read inside your browser tab. If OCR is needed, the engine and English data (about 7 MB) download once from this site; your document is never sent anywhere.

      Why does it say "(OCR)" next to some pages?

      Those pages had no usable text inside the PDF, usually because they were scanned, so the text was read from the page image. Check them for mistakes.

      Why is the extracted text in a strange order?

      PDFs store text by position, not by reading order. Single-column pages come out correctly; multi-column layouts, sidebars and tables can be mixed up. Try "OCR all pages" if the order is badly wrong.

      Does it work for languages other than English?

      Digital text in any language is copied exactly. OCR of scanned pages currently supports English only.

      Will it make my PDF searchable?

      No. This tool gives you a text file; it doesn't change the PDF. You can search the text file, or paste it wherever you need it.
