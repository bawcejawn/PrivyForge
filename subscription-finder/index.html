How to find your subscriptions in 3 steps

  1. Download a CSV of your transactions from your bank or card's website (see below). Six months catches weekly and monthly charges; 13 months or more also catches yearly ones.
  2. Drop the file on the box above. The date, description and amount columns are detected automatically. If they aren't, pick them from the dropdowns.
  3. Review the list. Each recurring charge shows how often it bills, the amount, what it costs per year and when the next charge is expected. Tick the ones you want to cancel to see your yearly savings, and export the list as a CSV if you want to keep it.

You can add several exports from the same account at once, for example months downloaded separately; they're combined into one list. Files from different banks usually have different column layouts, so check those one at a time. Subscriptions are often spread across a debit card, a credit card and PayPal, so it's worth checking each.

How to get your CSV

Almost every bank and card issuer lets you download transactions. Look for Download, Export or Statements in online banking, pick a date range of 3–12 months, and choose CSV (sometimes "Excel" or "spreadsheet"). PDF statements won't work yet.

Why this is safe to use

Most "subscription manager" apps ask you to link your bank login so they can read your transactions on their servers. This tool doesn't. Your file is opened by your own browser and analyzed in memory. Nothing is uploaded, stored or shared — you can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it still works.

How detection works

Charges are grouped by merchant (ignoring store numbers and reference codes), then checked for a regular rhythm — about 7, 14, 30, 91 or 365 days apart — and a consistent amount. Groceries and one-off purchases don't repeat that way, so they're left out. Price increases are flagged so you notice when a service quietly raises its rate.

Frequently asked questions

My bank uses separate debit and credit columns.

That's supported. If columns aren't detected correctly, choose them in the dropdowns that appear after loading.

Why is a subscription missing?

It needs at least two charges in your file. Yearly subscriptions need 13+ months of history. Charges billed through app stores or PayPal may show under that company's name.

Can I use a PDF statement instead?

Not yet. PDF statements are laid out for printing, not for reading row by row, so the tool needs a CSV export. Nearly every bank offers one under Download or Export.

Does it cancel subscriptions for me?

No — and it never touches your accounts. Use the "next expected" date to cancel before the next charge.

Is this financial advice?

No. The results are estimates based on the transactions in your file. Check your statements and each provider's terms before cancelling.
