How to use it

  1. Enter the vehicle's price and the cash you'll put down.
  2. If you're trading in a car, enter what the dealer will give you for it and what you still owe on it.
  3. Enter your sales tax rate and any dealer, title and registration fees you'll finance. Leave "Tax applies after trade-in credit" ticked if your state taxes the price minus your trade-in.
  4. Enter the APR from your loan offer and pick a term. The comparison table shows every term from 36 to 84 months at once; click a row to switch to it.

Worked example

A $30,000 car with $3,000 down. Your trade-in is worth $8,000 and you still owe $5,000 on it, so it's worth $3,000 of equity. Sales tax is 6% and fees are $500. With tax after the trade-in credit, tax is charged on $22,000: $1,320.

Amount financed = $30,000 + $1,320 tax + $500 fees − $3,000 down − $3,000 equity = $25,820.

At 6.5% APR the terms compare like this:

  • 36 months: $791.36 a month, $2,668.87 interest
  • 48 months: $612.32 a month, $3,571.38 interest
  • 60 months: $505.20 a month, $4,491.90 interest
  • 72 months: $434.03 a month, $5,430.40 interest
  • 84 months: $383.41 a month, $6,386.65 interest

Going from 48 to 72 months cuts the payment by $178.29 but adds $1,859.02 of interest — and that's before any rate difference, since lenders often charge more for longer terms. If your state taxed the full $30,000 instead, tax would be $1,800, you'd borrow $26,300, and the 60-month payment would be $514.59.

What "total cost" means here

Total cost is the cash you put down, plus the trade-in equity you hand over, plus every loan payment. It's what the car costs you, including tax, fees and interest. If you owe more on your trade-in than it's worth, that negative equity is added to the new loan, so it shows up in your payments and you pay interest on it.

How the payment is calculated

The payment is the standard fixed-rate loan payment, rounded to the cent. Each month's interest is the balance × APR ÷ 12, rounded to the cent, and the final payment is adjusted so the balance ends at exactly zero. Lenders that charge simple daily interest can differ by a few cents depending on when your payment arrives. Rebates, dealer add-ons, GAP coverage and warranties aren't included; add any you'll finance to fees.

Frequently asked questions

Is a longer car loan a bad idea?

It lowers the payment but costs more interest, and a long loan can leave you owing more than the car is worth for years. The comparison table shows exactly what each extra year costs at your rate.

Is sales tax charged before or after the trade-in?

It depends on your state. Many give a trade-in tax credit, so tax is charged on the price minus the trade-in; some don't. Check your state's motor vehicle or revenue department and use the toggle above.

Should I roll negative equity into the new loan?

You can, but you'll pay interest on debt for a car you no longer own. Paying it down first, or keeping the old car longer, often costs less. Enter both scenarios to compare.

APR or interest rate?

Enter the APR from your offer. For most car loans with no prepaid finance charges they're the same; if they differ, APR gives the fairer comparison between lenders.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything runs in your browser. Your inputs are kept only in the page link so you can bookmark them.
