Free image tools that run in your browser

Compress, convert and resize images without sending them anywhere. Batch as many as you like and download them all as a ZIP.

What these tools use under the hood

PrivyForge's free image tools run WebAssembly builds of well-known open-source encoders — MozJPEG for JPEG, libwebp for WebP, libavif for AVIF and OxiPNG for PNG — in background workers on your own device. That's the same family of codecs used by Google's Squoosh app. Because nothing is uploaded, there's no queue, no file-size cap and no waiting for a download.

How to compress, convert or resize images

  1. Add your images. Drop in as many as you like, click to choose them, or paste a screenshot.
  2. Pick an output format. "Keep original" compresses in the same format; choose WebP, AVIF, JPEG or PNG to convert.
  3. Set the quality. The default of 75 is a good balance; lower means smaller files.
  4. Resize if needed by entering a maximum width or height. Images keep their proportions and are never enlarged.
  5. Download each image, or all of them as one ZIP.

Example: shrinking phone photos for a website

A typical 12-megapixel phone photo is 4032 × 3024 pixels — far more than a web page needs. Set the format to WebP and the maximum width to 1600, and each photo comes out at 1600 × 1200 pixels, the same 4:3 shape with about one-sixth of the pixels. Click any thumbnail to compare before and after, then download the whole batch as a ZIP.

Choosing a format

  • JPEG: photos that must open everywhere, including older software and email clients.
  • WebP: the practical default for websites. Every current browser supports it, and files are usually noticeably smaller than JPEG at similar quality.
  • AVIF: often smaller still, but slower to encode. Good for sites that want the fastest pages.
  • PNG: logos, screenshots and graphics with sharp edges or transparency.

Privacy bonus: location data removed

Phone photos often include EXIF metadata such as the GPS coordinates where the picture was taken. Re-encoding an image here drops that metadata, so the compressed copy is safer to post or send. Photos are still rotated correctly, because the orientation is applied before the metadata is removed.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded?

No. They're processed by your browser on your device and never sent anywhere.

Will transparent PNGs stay transparent?

Yes, when you keep PNG or choose WebP or AVIF. JPEG has no transparency, so transparent areas become white.

Is there a limit on how many images I can process?

No. Images are processed in parallel on your device, so big batches just take a little longer.
