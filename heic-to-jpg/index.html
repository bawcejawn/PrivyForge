    How to convert HEIC to JPG

    1. Open this page in Safari on an iPhone, iPad or Mac. The box above tells you straight away whether your browser can read HEIC.
    2. Add your photos. Tap the box and pick photos from your library or the Files app. You can choose several at once.
    3. Pick quality and size. Quality 85 keeps photos looking like the originals. Set a max width if you want smaller files for email or a website.
    4. Download. Save each JPG, or use “Download all” to get a ZIP.

    Why this only works in Safari (for now)

    HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) stores photos using HEVC compression, the same technology as H.265 video. iPhones have saved photos as HEIC by default since iOS 11 because the files are much smaller than JPGs of similar quality.

    The catch is support. Safari includes a HEIC decoder because Apple’s operating systems do. Chrome, Firefox and Edge don’t, so they can’t open HEIC files at all. Most “HEIC to JPG” websites get around this by uploading your photos to their server and converting them there. We don’t upload anything, so this page relies on the decoder your browser already has, and it says so honestly instead of failing silently.

    If you’re on Windows, Android or Linux, the workarounds in the box above (switching the iPhone to “Most Compatible” or letting it convert automatically when copying to a computer) are the quickest fix, and they don’t send your photos anywhere either.

    A worked example: shrinking an iPhone photo for email

    A 12-megapixel iPhone photo is 4032×3024 pixels. Converting it at full size keeps every pixel, which is what you want for printing. For email or a website, set the max width to 2000: the photo is scaled to 2000×1500 (3024 × 2000 ÷ 4032 = 1500), which is about a quarter of the original pixel count (3.0 megapixels instead of 12.2), so the JPG is far smaller and still looks sharp on any screen. If a form demands a specific file size, use compress to 100 KB in Safari, which also accepts HEIC.

    Saving the JPGs on iPhone and iPad

    When you tap Download in Safari, the JPG goes to the Downloads folder in the Files app. To put it in your photo library, open it in Files, tap the Share button and choose Save Image. If you converted several photos, “Download all” saves a single ZIP; tap it in Files to unpack it. On a Mac, downloads land in your Downloads folder as usual.

    HEIC or JPG: which should you keep?

    Keep the HEIC originals in your photo library: they take up less space, and every conversion to JPG is a slightly lossy copy. Convert copies to JPG when you need to share them with something that can’t open HEIC, such as a Windows PC, an Android phone, an older printer kiosk, or an upload form that lists “JPG/JPEG only”. Converting is a one-way copy; your originals aren’t touched.

    What you get

    • A standard JPG that opens in Windows, Android, email, Office and every upload form.
    • Correct orientation. The rotation stored in the photo is applied, so portrait shots stay upright.
    • No location data. iPhone photos usually record where they were taken. The converted JPG doesn’t carry that metadata, which makes it safer to share.
    • The main image only. For Live Photos, you get the still photo, not the motion clip.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why doesn’t this work in Chrome, Firefox or Edge?

    Those browsers don’t include a HEIC decoder. Rather than upload your photos to a server to convert them, PrivyForge uses the browser’s own decoder, which only Safari has. Open the page in Safari, or set your iPhone camera to “Most Compatible”.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Photos are converted inside your browser and never leave your device. The page keeps working offline after the first visit.

    Will converting to JPG lower the quality?

    JPG compresses differently from HEIC, so files at the same quality are larger. At quality 85 the result looks like the original for normal viewing.

    Is the location removed from my photos?

    Yes. The converted JPG is a new image without the original metadata, so GPS coordinates aren’t included.

    How do I stop my iPhone taking HEIC photos?

    Open Settings → Camera → Formats and choose Most Compatible. New photos are saved as JPG; existing photos stay HEIC.
