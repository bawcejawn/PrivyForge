    How to convert HEIC to JPG

    1. Add your photos. Drop HEIC or HEIF files on the box above, or tap it to choose them. On an iPhone or iPad you can pick from your photo library or the Files app. You can add as many as you like.
    2. Wait a moment. Each photo is decoded and saved as a JPG on your device. Large photos take several seconds each, depending on your device.
    3. Pick quality and size. Quality 85 keeps photos looking like the originals. Set a max width if you want smaller files for email or a website. Changing a setting re-converts everything.
    4. Download. Save each JPG, or use “Download all” to get a ZIP.

    How it works without uploading your photos

    HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) stores photos using HEVC compression, the same technology as H.265 video. iPhones have saved photos as HEIC by default since iOS 11 because the files are much smaller than JPGs of similar quality. The catch is support: Windows, Android and many websites can’t open them.

    Safari can read HEIC itself, so in Safari this page uses the browser’s own decoder. Chrome, Edge and Firefox can’t, and many online converters work around that by uploading your photos to a server. PrivyForge doesn’t. Instead, the first time you add a HEIC photo in one of those browsers, the page loads an open-source HEIC decoder (libheif, compiled to WebAssembly) and runs it inside your browser. The download is about 0.8 MB compressed (3 MB unpacked), happens once, and contains no photo data in either direction: your photos are read from your disk and the JPGs are written back to it. Your browser keeps the decoder, so later conversions start straight away and also work offline.

    Pages that don’t involve HEIC never load the decoder, and neither do JPG or PNG files you add here or to the other image tools.

    A worked example: shrinking an iPhone photo for email

    A 12-megapixel iPhone photo is 4032×3024 pixels. Converting it at full size keeps every pixel, which is what you want for printing. For email or a website, set the max width to 2000: the photo is scaled to 2000×1500 (3024 × 2000 ÷ 4032 = 1500), which is about a quarter of the original pixel count (3.0 megapixels instead of 12.2), so the JPG is far smaller and still looks sharp on any screen. If a form demands a specific file size, use compress to 100 KB: it accepts HEIC photos too, in every browser.

    Saving the JPGs on iPhone and iPad

    When you tap Download in Safari, the JPG goes to the Downloads folder in the Files app. To put it in your photo library, open it in Files, tap the Share button and choose Save Image. If you converted several photos, “Download all” saves a single ZIP; tap it in Files to unpack it. On a Mac, downloads land in your Downloads folder as usual.

    HEIC or JPG: which should you keep?

    Keep the HEIC originals in your photo library: they take up less space, and every conversion to JPG is a slightly lossy copy. Convert copies to JPG when you need to share them with something that can’t open HEIC, such as a Windows PC, an Android phone, an older printer kiosk, or an upload form that lists “JPG/JPEG only”. Converting is a one-way copy; your originals aren’t touched.

    What you get

    • A standard JPG that opens in Windows, Android, email, Office and every upload form.
    • Correct orientation. The rotation stored in the photo is applied, so portrait shots stay upright.
    • No location data. iPhone photos usually record where they were taken. The converted JPG doesn’t carry that metadata, which makes it safer to share.
    • The main image only. For Live Photos you get the still photo, not the motion clip. If a HEIC file holds several images (some burst or edited photos do), you get its primary image, the one your phone shows.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does this work in Chrome, Edge and Firefox?

    Yes. Safari reads HEIC with its own decoder; in Chrome, Edge and Firefox the page loads PrivyForge’s built-in decoder the first time you add a HEIC photo (about 0.8 MB, once). Either way the conversion happens on your device.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Photos are converted inside your browser and never leave your device. The only download is the decoder itself, which is the same file for everyone and carries no photo data.

    Will converting to JPG lower the quality?

    JPG compresses differently from HEIC, so files at the same quality are larger. At quality 85 the result looks like the original for normal viewing.

    Is the location removed from my photos?

    Yes. The converted JPG is a new image without the original metadata, so GPS coordinates aren’t included.

    What if a photo won’t convert?

    You’ll see “Couldn’t read this HEIC photo” next to it. That usually means the file is damaged or only partly copied. Copy it from your phone again and retry. Very large panoramas can also run out of memory on phones; try a computer.

    How do I stop my iPhone taking HEIC photos?

    Open Settings → Camera → Formats and choose Most Compatible. New photos are saved as JPG; existing photos stay HEIC.
