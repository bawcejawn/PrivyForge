    Why sites ask for images under 100 KB

    Online application systems handle a large number of uploads, and small files keep them fast and cheap to store. That is why job applications, university and scholarship forms, government services, membership cards and many exam registration portals set limits such as 100 KB for a photo or a scanned document. The limit is a technical rule, not a quality judgment: a well-compressed 100 KB JPG still looks sharp on screen.

    Size is often not the only rule. Before you upload, check the form for:

    • Pixel dimensions (for example a minimum or exact width and height). If needed, set them first with the image resizer, then bring the result here.
    • Shape and cropping. This tool doesn’t crop. If a photo must be a particular shape, crop it in your phone’s photo editor first.
    • File type. If it says JPG/JPEG only, keep the JPG option.
    • Background or framing rules for ID-style photos. Compression doesn’t change what is in the picture.

    How the target size is reached

    File size depends on two things: how many pixels the image has, and the quality setting used to save it. This tool searches for the highest quality that fits first, because lowering quality a little is less noticeable than shrinking the picture:

    1. It tries quality 95. If that already fits, you get it.
    2. Otherwise it tries quality 50. If that fits, it narrows down between the two (a binary search) to find the highest quality that still fits, in at most eight more attempts.
    3. If even quality 50 is too big, it shrinks the dimensions step by step (to 85% each time, or more when the file is far too big) and searches again.

    The result is never over your limit. In the rare case the limit is impossible (for example 5 KB for a detailed photo), the tool says so and shows the smallest file it could make.

    Tips for the best result

    • Start from the original. A photo that has already been compressed several times has less detail left to work with.
    • Crop away empty space before compressing. Fewer pixels of background means more of the 100 KB goes to what matters.
    • Scanned documents: scan at a moderate resolution (around 150–200 DPI is usually plenty for reading on screen) rather than the scanner’s maximum.

    Frequently asked questions

    Will my photo be resized?

    Only if quality alone can’t get it under 100 KB. Large phone photos usually are scaled down; the final dimensions are shown next to each file.

    Does 100 KB mean 100,000 or 102,400 bytes?

    Sites differ. We aim for at most 100,000 bytes, which satisfies both definitions.

    Why is my file a bit under 100 KB rather than exactly 100 KB?

    Quality goes up in whole steps, and the next step up was over the limit. The tool always picks the highest quality that fits.

    Should I choose JPG or WebP?

    JPG for forms and portals, because many accept only JPG/JPEG. WebP gives smaller files for your own website.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Everything happens in your browser, so ID photos and documents never leave your device. The page works offline after the first visit.
