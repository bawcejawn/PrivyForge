How to use it

  1. Enter the balance and the purchase APR from your latest statement.
  2. Choose a fixed monthly payment, or switch to "be debt-free in a set number of months" to get the payment you need.
  3. Check the minimum payment formula against your statement. The defaults (1% of the balance plus interest, $25 floor) are a common shape, but issuers' formulas vary.
  4. Optionally tick "Compare a balance transfer" and enter the card's fee, promo rate, promo length and the rate after the promo.

Your inputs stay in the page link, so you can bookmark a scenario or save it to My PrivyForge.

Worked example

Say you owe $5,000 at 22.99% APR and pay $200 a month. The first month's interest is $95.79, so only about $104 of that payment reduces the balance. Keep going and the card is clear in 35 months, with $1,871.10 of interest.

Pay only the minimum (1% of the balance plus interest, $25 floor) and the first payment is $145.79 — but it shrinks as the balance does. That stretches payoff to 232 months (19 years 4 months) and $8,488.89 of interest: more than four times what the $200 plan costs.

Want it gone in two years instead? Switch the mode to 24 months and the calculator asks for $261.85 a month, for $1,284.17 of interest.

Now tick the balance transfer: a 3% fee ($150), 0% for 18 months, then 22.99%. At the same $200 a month, $1,550 is still owed when the promo ends, and the card is paid off in 27 months. Fee plus interest comes to $294.39 — about $1,577 less than staying put. The transfer only wins this clearly because most of the balance is paid during the promo; a smaller payment or a shorter promo narrows the gap, which is why the calculator shows the numbers instead of a rule.

How the math works

Each month, interest is the balance times the APR divided by 12, rounded to the cent. It's added to the balance, then your payment comes off. The last payment is just what's left, so nothing is overpaid. Card issuers actually charge interest daily on your average daily balance, and months have different lengths, so your statement can differ from this by a few cents to a few dollars a month. The payoff time and the comparison between plans hold up.

If your payment is less than or equal to the month's interest, the balance never falls. The calculator says so instead of showing a payoff date, and tells you the smallest payment that makes progress.

The estimate assumes no new purchases, fees or rate changes. If you're still using the card, those charges add to the time and cost.

Frequently asked questions

Why does paying the minimum take so long?

Because the minimum is mostly interest and falls as the balance falls. With a 1%-plus-interest formula, you only repay about 1% of what you owe each month until the floor kicks in.

Is a balance transfer always worth it?

No. You pay a fee up front (often 3–5% of the balance), and anything left when the promo ends is charged the regular rate. It works best when you can pay most of the balance during the promo. Enter the real offer terms above to check.

What APR should I enter?

The purchase APR on your statement. If part of the balance is a cash advance or already on a promo rate, the result is an approximation.

Should I pay this card or another debt first?

For several debts, try the debt payoff planner, which compares paying the highest rate first with paying the smallest balance first.

Is my data private?

Yes. Everything is calculated in your browser; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.
