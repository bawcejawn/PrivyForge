Convert JPG, PNG and WebP images to PDF

Put photos, scans and screenshots into one PDF, one image per page, in the order you choose, without uploading them anywhere.

Drop images here or click to choose JPG, PNG, WebP (also GIF, BMP, AVIF if your browser opens them) · drag rows to reorder (or Alt + ↑/↓)
    OrientationAutomatic: wide images get landscape pages

    How to turn images into a PDF

    1. Add your images. Drop them on the box, click to choose them, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl/Cmd + V. You can add more at any time.
    2. Put them in order. Drag rows up or down, or focus a row and press Alt + ↑/↓. The top image becomes page 1.
    3. Choose a page size. Fit to image makes each page exactly the shape of its picture. A4 and US Letter give you printable paper pages with the image scaled to fit and centred.
    4. Pick a margin if you want white space around each image, which helps when printing.
    5. Click "Create PDF & download". You get one PDF with one image per page.

    What the page-size options do

    Fit to image sizes each page at 96 pixels per inch, the standard screen size, plus any margin you choose. Screenshots and scans look exactly as they do on screen, with no white bars. Camera photos make large pages, which is normal: PDF readers zoom to fit, and the page dimensions don't change the file size.

    A4 and US Letter are for documents you will print or submit to an office. Each page turns to landscape when its image is wider than it is tall, and portrait otherwise. The image is shrunk to fit inside the margins, never cropped or stretched.

    Worked example

    Say you add a 4000 × 3000 pixel phone photo and choose US Letter with a medium (½ inch) margin. The photo is wider than tall, so the page becomes landscape: 11 × 8.5 inches. Removing ½ inch on every side leaves a 10 × 7.5 inch area. The photo has the same 4:3 shape, so it fills that area exactly. 4000 pixels across 10 inches is 400 pixels per inch, well above the roughly 300 that print shops ask for. That photo will print sharply.

    With A4 and the same margin, the usable area is 10.69 × 7.27 inches. The photo is limited by height, so it prints at 9.69 × 7.27 inches, about 413 pixels per inch, with a little extra white space left and right.

    With Fit to image and no margin, the same photo makes a 41.7 × 31.3 inch page. It looks normal on screen; only the page's nominal size is large.

    Quality: what happens to each format

    • JPG/JPEG photos are placed in the PDF as they are, with no re-compression and no quality loss. If your phone saved the photo sideways with an EXIF "rotate" tag, the tool rotates it on the page so it shows upright, without re-encoding it. Mirrored JPEGs, which are rare, are redrawn first.
    • PNG images are stored losslessly, and transparent areas stay transparent, so they show white on a normal page.
    • WebP and other formats can't go into a PDF directly, so they are drawn in your browser and saved as high-quality JPEG (quality 92) on a white background. Very large images are scaled down to 16 megapixels to stay within mobile browser limits.

    The PDF is roughly the size of your images added together. To make it smaller for email, run it through Compress PDF or shrink the photos first with Compress Image.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. The PDF is assembled in your browser with pdf-lib. You can switch off Wi-Fi after the page loads and it keeps working.

    Does converting JPG to PDF reduce quality?

    No. JPEG data is copied into the PDF unchanged, and PNGs are stored losslessly. Only formats a PDF can't hold, such as WebP, are converted, at high JPEG quality.

    Why is my iPhone photo (HEIC) not accepted?

    HEIC isn't one of the formats this page accepts, because most browsers can't open it. When you choose photos through the file picker on an iPhone, it usually hands the browser a JPEG copy. On a computer, export the photo as JPEG first, or set your iPhone's Settings › Camera › Formats to "Most Compatible" so new photos are saved as JPEG.

    Can I put several images on one page?

    Not yet. Each image becomes its own page. To combine finished PDFs, use Merge PDF.

    Is there a limit on how many images I can add?

    No fixed limit. The only limit is your device's memory, because every image is held in memory while the PDF is built.
