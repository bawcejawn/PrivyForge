How to use it

  1. Pick what you want to know: how your savings grow, how long until you reach a goal, or how much to save each month to reach it by a date.
  2. Enter your starting balance and monthly deposit.
  3. Enter the rate. Savings accounts usually advertise an APY, so that's the default. If you only have an APR, switch to APR and choose how often it compounds.
  4. Optionally raise your contribution each year — for example by 3% to keep pace with raises.

Worked examples

A lump sum. $10,000 at 5% APY for 10 years grows to $16,288.95 — that's $10,000 × 1.0510. At 5% APR compounded daily it would be $16,486.65, because daily compounding turns a 5% APR into about a 5.127% APY.

Regular saving. Start with $10,000, add $200 a month, and earn 4.5% APY. After 10 years you have $45,624.83: $34,000 you put in and $11,624.83 of interest. Raise the deposit 3% each year and it becomes $49,662.57 ($37,513.31 in, $12,149.26 interest).

A goal. To turn $5,000 into $50,000 in 10 years at 4.5% APY, you'd need to save $280.68 a month. Or flip it around: saving $300 a month gets you there in 9 years 7 months.

APY vs APR

APR (annual percentage rate) is the nominal yearly rate before compounding. APY (annual percentage yield) is what you actually earn in a year once interest earns interest: APY = (1 + APR ÷ n)n − 1, where n is the number of times it compounds per year. A 4.5% APR compounded monthly is a 4.594% APY; compounded daily it's 4.602%. APY already includes compounding, so when you enter an APY, the compounding frequency doesn't matter — which is why US banks quote APY for deposit accounts: it lets you compare them directly.

How the calculator works

Each month the balance grows by (1 + APY)1/12, then that month's deposit is added. The year-by-year table and chart show how much of the balance is your own money and how much is interest. Interest isn't rounded month by month the way a bank posts it, so a real account can differ by a few cents. Rates on savings accounts are variable and can change at any time; taxes on interest aren't included.

Not financial advice. These are projections at a fixed rate; real rates change and past rates don't predict future ones.

Frequently asked questions

Does compounding frequency matter much?

Less than people think. On a 4.5% APR, daily instead of monthly compounding adds less than 0.01 percentage points of yield. The rate itself and how much you deposit matter far more.

Should I enter APY or APR?

Whatever your bank quotes. For savings accounts and CDs that's usually APY. If you have an APR, pick APR and the compounding frequency, and the calculator converts it.

When are deposits added?

At the end of each month, so a deposit starts earning the following month. Depositing at the start of the month would earn slightly more.

Can I use it for investments?

You can enter an expected return, but investments don't grow at a fixed rate and can lose value. Treat any result as one scenario, not a forecast.

Is my data private?

Yes. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a projection tool; check account terms and current rates with the bank.
