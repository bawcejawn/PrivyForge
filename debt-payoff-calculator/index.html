How to use the debt payoff calculator

  1. List each debt with its current balance, APR and minimum monthly payment. Your statements show all three. Use "+ Add a debt" for more rows and ✕ to remove one.
  2. Enter your total monthly budget for debt. It must at least cover the minimums, which are added up for you under the table.
  3. Compare the strategies. The table shows avalanche, snowball and minimums-only side by side, and the chart shows your total debt falling over time.
  4. Follow the payoff order listed under the table, and bookmark the page: your plan is saved in the link.

Worked example: four debts, $900 a month

The default plan has a credit card ($6,500 at 24.99%, $180 minimum), a store card ($1,200 at 29.99%, $45), a car loan ($14,000 at 7.4%, $320) and a personal loan ($4,000 at 12.5%, $135). The minimums add up to $680, and the budget is $900, so $220 a month goes on top.

  • Avalanche: debt-free in 2 yr 10 mo, with $4,612 in total interest.
  • Snowball: debt-free in 2 yr 11 mo, with $5,021 in interest — $409 more.
  • Minimums only: 5 yr 8 mo and $9,654 in interest. Avalanche saves $5,043 compared with this.

Both strategies clear the store card first (month 5), because it has the highest rate and the smallest balance. Then they split: avalanche goes after the 24.99% credit card, while snowball knocks out the $4,000 personal loan by month 15.

Raise the budget to $1,000 and avalanche finishes in 2 yr 6 mo with $3,899 in interest. Every extra dollar goes straight to the target debt.

Snowball or avalanche: which is better?

Avalanche sends every spare dollar to the debt with the highest interest rate. Mathematically it costs the least interest. Snowball pays off the smallest balance first. It usually costs a little more, but many people find that clearing a whole debt quickly keeps them motivated. This calculator shows both so you can see what that motivation costs in dollars — and the best plan is the one you'll stick with.

How it works

  1. Each month, interest is added to every balance (APR ÷ 12, rounded to the cent).
  2. Every debt gets its minimum payment.
  3. Whatever is left of your budget goes to the target debt. When a debt is paid off, its minimum "rolls over" to the next one.

Real statements can differ a little: card issuers usually charge interest daily, and minimum payments on cards often shrink as the balance falls. Treat the dates as close estimates.

Frequently asked questions

What if my budget only covers minimums?

You'll see the minimums-only result. If a minimum doesn't cover the monthly interest, that debt never gets paid off, and the calculator tells you so.

Should I consolidate instead?

A consolidation loan or 0% balance transfer helps when the new rate is clearly below your current blended rate and fees are low. Add it as a single debt at the new rate to compare.

Can I include a mortgage or student loan?

Yes. Add it like any other debt. Low-rate loans like these usually come last under avalanche.

Is my information saved?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser. Your numbers appear in the page link only so you can bookmark your plan.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's an estimate for planning; your lenders' statements are authoritative.
