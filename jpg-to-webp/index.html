Convert JPG to WebP — free, private, in your browser

Turn JPG photos into smaller WebP files for your website or app. Conversion happens on your device, so your photos are never uploaded.

    How to convert JPG to WebP

    1. Add your JPGs. Drag them onto the box, click to browse, or paste an image with Ctrl/Cmd+V. You can add as many as you like.
    2. Leave quality at 80, or adjust it. Every image converts immediately and re-converts when you move the slider, so you can watch the file size change.
    3. Optionally set a max width. If your site never shows the photo wider than, say, 1600 pixels, there is no reason to ship a 4000-pixel file.
    4. Download. Save each WebP on its own, or click “Download all” for a ZIP.

    A worked example

    We ran our 2400×1600 test photo, a 368 KB JPG, through this page at the default quality of 80. The WebP came out at 79 KB, 79% smaller, with the same dimensions. Setting a max width of 1600 pixels as well brought it down to 49 KB at 1600×1067. Your own results will differ: a photo of clear sky shrinks far more than a photo of gravel, because fine random detail is the hardest thing for any encoder to compress.

    Why convert JPG to WebP?

    WebP is an image format from Google that compresses photos more efficiently than JPG. Google’s own comparison study reported WebP files around 25–34% smaller than JPGs of similar visual quality. Smaller images mean pages that load faster, use less of your visitors’ mobile data, and score better on page-speed tools, where the largest image on the page is often what holds the score back.

    Every current major browser displays WebP: Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari (since Safari 14). For a website, WebP is a safe default today.

    When to keep JPG instead

    • Upload forms that only accept JPG. Many job, government and exam portals still reject WebP. For those, use compress to 100 KB or a similar target-size tool, which outputs JPG.
    • Printing and photo labs. Print services and older desktop software expect JPG.
    • Sending photos to people. A JPG opens everywhere, including older email clients and image viewers.

    Choosing a quality setting

    Quality is a trade-off between file size and fine detail. Around 75–85 is hard to tell apart from the original for most photos; 80 is the default here. Drop to 60–70 for thumbnails or background images where size matters most. Above 90, files grow quickly for little visible gain. Click any thumbnail to open a before/after slider and judge for yourself before downloading.

    Converting a whole folder

    Select every JPG in a folder at once (Ctrl/Cmd+A in the file picker) or drag the whole selection onto the box. Images are converted in parallel on your processor’s cores, and the settings apply to all of them. When they’re done, “Download all” gives you a single ZIP with the same file names and a .webp extension, ready to upload to your site.

    Using WebP on your website

    If you need to support very old browsers too, serve the WebP with a JPG fallback using the <picture> element. Browsers pick the first format they understand:

    <picture>
  <source srcset="photo.webp" type="image/webp">
  <img src="photo.jpg" alt="Describe the photo" width="1600" height="1067">
</picture>

    Most content management systems and image plugins can also do this for you once the WebP files exist.

    Private by design

    Most online converters upload your photos to a server, convert them there, and send them back. This page uses the libwebp encoder compiled to WebAssembly and runs it inside your browser tab, so the photos never leave your device. Converting also drops the photo’s EXIF metadata, including any GPS location your phone recorded. After the first visit, the page keeps working with your internet switched off.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Conversion runs inside your browser with WebAssembly. Your photos stay on your device, and the page works offline once loaded.

    Is the WebP lossy or lossless?

    This tool makes lossy WebP files, which is what gives the big size savings for photos. The quality slider controls how much detail is kept.

    Is there a limit on file size or number of images?

    No daily limits, no file caps and no watermarks. The only limit is your device’s memory; very large photos can be slow on older phones.

    Will the WebP have the same dimensions?

    Yes, unless you set a max width. Images are never enlarged.

    Can I convert WebP back to JPG, or PNG to WebP?

    Yes. Use the image compressor and choose the output format you need.
