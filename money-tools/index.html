Where to start

  1. Find money you're already losing. Export a few months of transactions from your bank as CSV and run the Subscription Finder. It lists every recurring charge and what it costs per year.
  2. Point that money at your most expensive debt. The Debt Payoff Planner compares the avalanche (highest rate first) and snowball (smallest balance first) methods and shows your debt-free date.
  3. Check your biggest loan. If you have a mortgage, see whether a refinance pays for itself before you'd move, or what extra payments do in the mortgage calculator.

How accurate are these calculators?

Loan schedules follow the convention US lenders use on statements: the monthly payment is rounded to the cent, each month's interest is rounded on the remaining balance, and the final payment is adjusted to clear the loan. The engine is tested against published examples, including a Consumer Financial Protection Bureau sample loan and the Texas Instruments BA II Plus guidebook.

They're still estimates. Your lender's disclosures and statements are the final word.

Is my financial information stored?

No. Calculations run on your device. Calculator inputs are kept in the page address only so you can bookmark or share a scenario, and bank CSV files are read in memory and never sent anywhere.

Not financial advice. These tools provide estimates for information only.
