Free trials that quietly turn into paid plans, a streaming service you signed up for one show, an app you forgot was billing yearly — recurring charges are easy to start and easy to forget. Finding them takes about half an hour, and you don't need to give any app your bank login to do it.

## Step 1: Download your transactions

Log in to each bank account and credit card you use and look for **Download**, **Export** or **Statements**. Choose:

- **Format:** CSV (sometimes called "Excel" or "spreadsheet"). PDF statements are harder to analyze.
- **Date range:** at least three months, ideally twelve. Monthly charges show up in three months; quarterly and yearly ones need a longer window.

Do this for every card, not just your main one. Subscriptions often live on an old card or a PayPal account.

## Step 2: Let a tool find the repeating charges

You can scan a statement by eye, but it's slow and easy to miss things billed once a year. The [PrivyForge Subscription Finder](/subscription-finder/) reads your CSV **inside your browser** — the file isn't uploaded anywhere — and:

1. groups charges by merchant, ignoring store numbers and reference codes (so "NETFLIX.COM 866-579…" and "NETFLIX.COM 866-580…" count as one);
2. checks whether they repeat on a regular rhythm — weekly, every two weeks, monthly, quarterly or yearly;
3. checks that the amounts are consistent, so groceries and one-off purchases are left out;
4. flags any service whose price changed between charges;
5. totals what each one costs per year.

Click **Try it with sample data** first if you'd like to see what the result looks like before using your own statement.

## Step 3: Decide what to keep

Look at the **per year** column. A $12.99 monthly charge feels small; $155.88 a year is easier to judge. For each item, ask:

- **Did I use this in the last month?** If not, it's a strong cancel candidate.
- **Do I have it twice?** Families often end up paying for the same service on two accounts, or for cloud storage from two providers.
- **Is there a cheaper plan?** Many services have ad-supported or annual plans that cost less than monthly billing.
- **Did the price change?** A flagged price change is a good moment to reconsider.

Tick the ones you want to cancel; the tool totals what you'd save per year.

## Step 4: Cancel before the next charge

Use the **next expected** date for each subscription and cancel a few days before it. Common routes:

- **Directly with the service:** account settings → subscription or billing → cancel.
- **App store subscriptions:** charges that appear as Apple or Google are managed in your phone's subscription settings, not on the service's website.
- **PayPal:** automatic payments can be stopped in PayPal's settings.
- **If a service makes cancelling hard:** contact your card issuer and ask about stopping the recurring charge. Keep any cancellation confirmation emails.

After cancelling, export the [subscription list as a CSV](/subscription-finder/) or note the dates, and check your next statement to make sure the charge actually stopped.

## Step 5: Put the money to work

The savings only matter if they don't get absorbed by new spending. Two common choices:

- send the freed-up amount to your highest-interest debt (see [Debt Snowball vs Avalanche](/blog/debt-snowball-vs-avalanche/)), or
- set up an automatic transfer of that amount into savings on the day the old charge used to hit.

## Why not use an app that links to your bank?

Subscription-tracking apps can be convenient, but most work by connecting to your bank account through a data aggregator, which means your full transaction history is shared with another company. A CSV you analyze yourself shares nothing. If you prefer the convenience of a linked app, check its privacy policy and what it does with your data.

## Common questions

**My bank only gives PDF statements.** Look for a "transactions" or "activity" export rather than "statements" — many banks offer CSV there. If not, a credit card or budgeting export may be available instead.

**A subscription I know about isn't listed.** It needs at least two charges in your file, and yearly subscriptions need more than twelve months of history. Charges billed through an app store or PayPal appear under that company's name.

**Is my statement stored anywhere?** No. It's read into your browser's memory and analyzed there. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool still works.
