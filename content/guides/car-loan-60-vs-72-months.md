Longer car loans are tempting because they shrink the monthly payment. The trade-off is less visible: you pay interest for longer, and the loan balance stays high while the car loses value. Here's what that looks like with real numbers.

## The example

A $35,000 car with $4,000 down leaves **$31,000 to finance**. To keep the comparison clean, we've left out sales tax, fees and any trade-in (the [car loan calculator](/car-loan-calculator/) handles all three). At the same 6.9% APR for every term:

| Term | Monthly payment | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 48 months | $740.90 | $4,563.01 |
| 60 months | $612.38 | $5,742.46 |
| 72 months | $527.03 | $6,946.34 |
| 84 months | $466.36 | $8,174.15 |

Going from 60 to 72 months saves **$85.35 a month** but adds **$1,203.88 in interest**. Stretching to 84 months adds another $1,227.81 on top of that.

## When the longer term also has a higher rate

Some lenders price longer terms at a higher rate. If the 72-month loan were 7.9% instead of 6.9%, the numbers move again:

- 72 months at 7.9%: **$542.02 a month, $8,025.23 interest** — $2,282.77 more interest than 60 months at 6.9%, for a payment only $70.36 lower.

That's why it's worth asking the lender for the rate on each term, not just the payment.

## The part people miss: how much you still owe

After three years of payments on the same $31,000 at 6.9%, the remaining balance is:

- 60-month loan: **$13,691.14**
- 72-month loan: **$17,094.08**
- 84-month loan: **$19,513.00**

Cars lose value, and if the balance falls more slowly than the car's value, you can end up owing more than the car is worth ("negative equity"). That matters if the car is totalled, or if you want to trade it in early — the shortfall usually gets rolled into the next loan.

## When a longer term can make sense

- The lower payment is genuinely what makes the budget work, and you plan to keep the car well past the loan term.
- You'll pay extra whenever you can. A longer term with extra payments gives flexibility; the calculator shows how extra payments shorten it.
- The rate is the same (or 0% promotional) across terms.

## How to decide

1. Start with the total price you can afford, not the monthly payment the dealer quotes.
2. Get a pre-approved rate from a bank or credit union before visiting the dealer, so you have a comparison.
3. Run your numbers in the [car loan calculator](/car-loan-calculator/): add tax, fees and any trade-in, then compare 48, 60 and 72 months side by side.
4. If the 72-month payment is the only one that fits, consider a less expensive car or a larger down payment instead.

## Common questions

**Is a 72-month car loan bad?**
Not automatically, but it costs more in interest and keeps you owing more for longer. Compare the total interest and the balance after a few years, not just the payment.

**Does paying extra on a car loan help?**
Usually yes: extra payments go to principal and cut future interest. Check that your loan has no prepayment penalty.

**Are these numbers what I'll actually pay?**
They're estimates for a simple-interest loan with monthly payments. Your lender's disclosure is the final word.
