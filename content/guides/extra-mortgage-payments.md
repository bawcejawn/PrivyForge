Every extra dollar you pay toward a mortgage's principal stops earning interest for the rest of the loan. On a 30-year mortgage, that compounding works in your favour for decades, which is why small extra payments add up to large savings.

## The example

A **$360,000 mortgage at 6.5% for 30 years** has a principal-and-interest payment of **$2,275.44** a month and costs **$459,164.00** in interest over the full term. Here's what extra monthly principal does, calculated with the same cent-accurate schedule our [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator/) uses:

| Extra each month | Interest saved | Paid off sooner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $100 | $62,909.75 | 41 months (3 yr 5 mo) |
| $250 | $127,718.69 | 86 months (7 yr 2 mo) |
| $500 | $196,133.47 | 135 months (11 yr 3 mo) |

A one-time **$10,000 extra payment at the end of year one** saves **$51,059.13** in interest and takes 26 months off the loan.

## Why it works

Each month, interest is charged on the balance you still owe. An extra payment lowers that balance immediately, so every later month's interest is a little smaller — and more of your regular payment goes to principal. The effect is largest early in the loan, when the balance (and the interest portion of each payment) is highest.

## Extra payments vs a 15-year mortgage

A 15-year mortgage forces faster payoff and usually comes with a lower rate. For comparison, the same $360,000 at 6.0% over 15 years costs **$3,037.88** a month and **$186,819.78** in interest. Paying the 30-year loan with $500 extra brings interest down to $263,030.53 — still about $76,000 more than the 15-year loan, but with a required payment that's $762.44 lower, which is a useful safety net if money gets tight. The 15-year loan wins on cost; the 30-year-plus-extra approach wins on flexibility, but only if you actually make the extra payments.

## Before you prepay, check these

- **High-interest debt first.** Paying off a 24% credit card beats prepaying a 6.5% mortgage. See [Debt Snowball vs Avalanche](/blog/debt-snowball-vs-avalanche/).
- **An emergency fund.** Money sent to the mortgage is hard to get back out.
- **Employer retirement match.** Missing out on a match can cost more than prepaying saves.
- **Prepayment rules.** Many mortgages have no prepayment penalty, but check your loan documents, and make sure extra money is applied to principal.

## How to do it

1. Ask your servicer how to make principal-only payments (often a separate field in online payments).
2. Pick an amount you can sustain, or round your payment up (for example from $2,275.44 to $2,400).
3. Model it first: enter your loan in the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator/) and set "Extra principal" to see your new payoff date.

## Common questions

**Is it better to invest instead?**
It depends on your mortgage rate, expected investment returns, taxes and risk tolerance. Prepaying gives a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate; investing may earn more or less.

**Does an extra payment lower my monthly payment?**
Not usually — it shortens the loan. Some lenders offer a "recast" that lowers the payment after a large lump sum, sometimes for a fee.

**Are biweekly payments the same thing?**
Paying half the monthly amount every two weeks results in 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments a year — effectively one extra payment annually.

*Not financial advice. These are estimates; your lender's statements are authoritative.*
