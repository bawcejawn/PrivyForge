Savings accounts usually advertise an **APY**; loans usually advertise an **APR**. They sound similar, but they measure different things, and mixing them up makes comparisons misleading.

## The short version

- **APR (annual percentage rate)** is the yearly rate *before* compounding.
- **APY (annual percentage yield)** is what you actually earn in a year *after* compounding.

When interest compounds more than once a year, the APY is higher than the APR. For a 4.5% APR:

| Compounding | APY |
| --- | --- |
| Annually | 4.5000% |
| Quarterly | 4.5765% |
| Monthly | 4.5940% |
| Daily | 4.6025% |

The difference from compounding frequency is small. The difference between accounts is not.

## Why the rate matters more than compounding

$10,000 left for five years:

- at **4.5% APY** grows to **$12,461.82** — $2,461.82 of interest;
- at **0.5% APY** grows to **$10,252.51** — $252.51 of interest.

Same money, same five years, roughly ten times the interest. That's why comparing the APY of the account you're in with what's available elsewhere is usually worth a few minutes.

## Regular contributions

Rates matter even more when you add money every month. Starting with $1,000 and adding **$200 a month** for 10 years at 4.5% APY:

- you contribute **$25,000**,
- interest adds **$6,648.10**,
- for a final balance of **$31,648.10**.

## Planning for a goal

The [compound interest calculator](/compound-interest-calculator/) also works backwards:

- **How long?** Saving $300 a month at 4.5% APY reaches **$10,000 in 32 months**.
- **How much?** To reach **$20,000 in 3 years** at 4.5% APY, you'd need to save about **$520.64 a month**.

## How to compare savings accounts

1. Compare **APY to APY** — it already includes compounding.
2. Check whether the rate is promotional and for how long.
3. Look for minimum balances or fees that would reduce what you earn.
4. Check that the account is covered by deposit insurance (FDIC in the US, or NCUA for credit unions).

## Common questions

**Is APY guaranteed?**
Not on most savings accounts: variable rates can change at any time. Certificates of deposit (CDs) usually lock a rate for a term.

**Why do loans advertise APR?**
In the US, lenders must disclose APR, which also folds in certain fees. A loan's APR is the better comparison between loan offers.

**Is interest on savings taxable?**
In the US, interest is generally taxable income. Check the rules for your situation.
