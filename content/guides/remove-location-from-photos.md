Most phone cameras can save the **exact location** where a photo was taken inside the image file, along with the date, time and phone model. That information is invisible when you look at the picture, but anyone who gets the original file can read it. Here's how to check a photo, remove the data, and stop it being saved in the first place.

## What's hidden in a photo file

Photos carry *metadata*, extra information stored alongside the picture. The common kind is called **EXIF**, and it can include:

- **GPS coordinates**, often precise enough to identify a building;
- the **date and time** the photo was taken;
- the **camera or phone make and model**, lens, and settings;
- the **software** that edited it;
- sometimes a **serial number** or owner name set in the camera.

Not every photo has all of these. It depends on your device and its settings. The only way to know is to look.

## Does a website remove it for me?

Some websites and apps strip location data when you upload a photo, and some don't. Their behaviour can also differ between posting publicly, sending a private message and sending "as a file". Email attachments, cloud-storage links, file transfers and marketplace listings may pass the original file along untouched. If it matters, remove the data yourself before the photo leaves your device.

## Remove location and metadata in your browser

1. Open [Remove Photo Metadata](/remove-photo-metadata/).
2. Drop in one or more photos (JPG, PNG, WebP or iPhone HEIC).
3. Each photo gets a card listing what was found. If it has a location, you'll see the coordinates highlighted.
4. Download the clean copy, or all of them as a ZIP.

The photos are processed on your own device and never uploaded. For JPG, PNG and WebP files the picture itself isn't re-compressed, so there's **no loss of quality**. The tool keeps the two things you'd want to keep: the photo's **rotation**, so it doesn't appear sideways, and its **colour profile**, so the colours don't shift. iPhone HEIC photos are converted to a clean JPG.

## Stop your phone saving location in the first place

**iPhone:** Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Camera → **Never**. New photos won't record where they were taken. Photos you already have keep their location.

**iPhone, one photo at a time:** when sharing from the Photos app, tap **Options** at the top of the share sheet and turn off **Location** before you send.

**Android:** most camera apps have a setting with a name like "Location tags", "Save location" or "Geotagging" in the camera's settings menu. The name and place vary by phone maker.

Turning off location means you lose the handy "photos on a map" view in your gallery for new photos. Many people keep it on and strip location only from photos they share. That's what the tool above is for.

## What metadata removal can't do

Removing metadata doesn't change what's *in* the picture. A street sign, house number, licence plate, school logo or a distinctive view out of a window can reveal a location just as clearly. Check the background before you post.

## Common questions

**Does removing metadata reduce photo quality?**
Not with this tool for JPG, PNG and WebP: the image data is copied exactly. HEIC photos are converted to JPG, which re-compresses them at high quality.

**Will the photo still be the right way up?**
Yes. If a photo relies on its metadata to display upright, a tiny orientation note is kept and everything else is removed.

**What about screenshots?**
Screenshots usually don't contain GPS data, but they can still contain other metadata. It's quick to check.

**Is it legal to remove metadata from my photos?**
For your own photos, generally yes. Metadata isn't the same as copyright; removing it doesn't change who owns the photo.
