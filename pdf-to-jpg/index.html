      How to convert a PDF to JPG

      1. Add your PDF by dropping it on the box or clicking to choose it.
      2. Choose a resolution. 150 DPI suits most uses. Pick 300 DPI for printing or zooming into small text.
      3. Set the JPEG quality if you want to. 85 is a good balance; higher means sharper text edges and bigger files.
      4. Choose pages (optional). Leave the box empty for every page, or type ranges like 1-3, 7 or 5- (page 5 to the end).
      5. Click "Convert & download". One page downloads as a JPG; several pages download as a ZIP. Previews appear below the button, and clicking one saves just that page.

      Resolution: what the DPI numbers mean

      A PDF page has a physical size, and DPI (dots per inch) sets how many pixels each inch becomes. These are the exact image sizes you'll get:

      SettingUS Letter (8.5 × 11 in)A4 (210 × 297 mm)
      72 DPI612 × 792 px595 × 842 px
      150 DPI1,275 × 1,650 px1,240 × 1,754 px
      300 DPI2,550 × 3,300 px2,480 × 3,508 px

      Doubling the DPI makes four times as many pixels, so files grow fast. A 300 DPI Letter page is 8.4 megapixels, four times a 150 DPI page. Use 300 DPI when the image will be printed or when you need to zoom in on fine detail such as a signature or a chart label. For a slide, a website, a chat message or an email, 150 DPI is plenty and much smaller.

      Worked example: very large pages

      To stay within what phones and tablets can handle, a page is never drawn larger than 16 megapixels. Mobile Safari refuses canvases much bigger than that. Ordinary pages never hit this limit. A tabloid page (11 × 17 in) at 300 DPI would be 3,300 × 5,100 = 16.8 megapixels, just over the limit, so it is drawn at 3,218 × 4,973 pixels instead (about 293 DPI). The status line tells you when this happens. Engineering drawings and posters are scaled further, keeping their proportions.

      What's inside the JPGs

      Each page is drawn by pdf.js, the PDF engine built into Firefox, onto a white background. Text, vector graphics, images and form fields that are visible on the page all appear in the picture. Transparent areas turn white because JPG has no transparency. The images are only pictures: text in them can't be selected or searched. Files are named after your PDF with the page number, for example report-page-03.jpg, so they sort in the right order.

      Going the other way? Image to PDF turns JPGs back into one PDF, and Split PDF extracts pages while keeping them as real PDF pages.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is my PDF uploaded?

      No. Rendering and JPEG encoding happen inside your browser tab, and the files are saved straight to your device. The page works offline after it has loaded once.

      Which resolution should I choose?

      150 DPI for screens, slides and email; 300 DPI for printing or reading fine print; 72 DPI for small previews or thumbnails.

      Why are my images blurry when I zoom in?

      A JPG has a fixed number of pixels. Convert again at 300 DPI and a higher quality setting for sharper text.

      Can I convert a password-protected PDF?

      No. Open it in your PDF reader, save an unlocked copy, and convert that.

      Can I get PNG instead of JPG?

      Not on this page yet. JPG keeps files small for scans and photos. If you need PNG, convert the JPGs with our image tool; note that this won't restore detail the JPEG step removed.
