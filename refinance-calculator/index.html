How to use the refinance calculator

  1. Enter your current loan: the remaining balance and rate from your latest statement, and how many years are left.
  2. Enter the new loan: the rate and term from a lender's quote, plus closing costs and any points.
  3. Decide how to pay the costs. Tick "Roll costs into the new loan" if you'd add them to the balance instead of paying cash at closing.
  4. Say how long you'll keep the home. This is the most important input: a refinance only pays off if you stay past break-even.
  5. Read the verdict. It tells you when you break even and how far ahead or behind you'll be when you expect to move.

Worked example: 7.25% down to 6.0%

The default inputs describe a $320,000 balance at 7.25% with 27 years left, refinanced into a 30-year loan at 6.0% with $6,500 in closing costs paid at closing, for someone staying 7 more years.

  • Payment: $2,253.42 now, $1,918.56 after refinancing, which is $334.86 less each month.
  • Break-even: 1 yr 8 mo.
  • After 7 years: $19,890 ahead.
  • Lifetime interest: $410,109 on the current loan vs $370,683 on the new one. The new loan also adds 3 years to your payoff.

Roll the $6,500 into the loan instead, and the new payment is $1,957.53. Break-even moves to 1 yr 10 mo and you're $17,289 ahead after 7 years: less cash up front, a little less benefit.

Choose a 15-year term at the same rate, and the payment rises to $2,700.34, which is $446.92 more a month. But far more of each payment goes to principal: break-even comes after 1 yr 6 mo, you're $35,583 ahead after 7 years, and the loan is gone 12 years sooner than your current one.

How we decide if a refinance is worth it

Lower monthly payments alone can mislead: a new 30-year loan restarts the clock, and closing costs are real money. So this calculator compares the two paths the way your net worth sees them — everything you've paid so far plus what you still owe, month by month. The break-even point is the first month the refinance path is ahead. If you plan to move or sell before then, refinancing loses money.

Rules of thumb

  • The bigger the rate drop and the longer you'll stay, the more likely a refinance pays off. Check the break-even rather than relying on a fixed rate gap.
  • Refinancing into a shorter term (30 → 15) usually raises the payment but saves the most interest.
  • Rolling costs into the loan keeps cash in your pocket but means paying interest on those costs.

Frequently asked questions

What are points?

Prepaid interest: each point costs 1% of the loan and lowers your rate. Enter them here to see if buying down the rate pays off within your timeline.

Why can a higher payment still come out ahead?

The comparison counts what you've paid plus what you still owe. A shorter loan at a lower rate pays down principal much faster, so you build equity sooner even though the payment is bigger.

Does this include PMI, taxes and insurance?

No. Taxes and insurance usually don't change when you refinance. If refinancing removes PMI, add that monthly amount to your savings mentally — or reduce the new rate slightly to approximate it.

Is my data private?

Yes. Calculations run in your browser; numbers stay in the page link only so you can bookmark them.

Is this financial advice?

No. Use it to plan, then compare official Loan Estimates from lenders.
