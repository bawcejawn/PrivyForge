Rent vs buy calculator

What this calculator counts

  • Owning: mortgage payment, property tax, insurance, maintenance, HOA, about 3% closing costs to buy and 6% to sell, and home appreciation.
  • Renting: rent rising each year and renter's insurance. The renter invests the down payment and closing costs on day one.
  • The difference: each month, whichever option is cheaper invests the savings at your chosen return. That's what makes the comparison fair.

Buying usually loses in the first few years (closing and selling costs are steep) and gains over time as the loan is paid down and the home appreciates. The faster rents rise, and the longer you stay, the better buying looks.

Frequently asked questions

Does it include tax deductions?

No. Since the 2018 tax changes most homeowners take the standard deduction, so the mortgage-interest deduction often doesn't apply. If you itemize, buying is slightly better than shown.

What return should I use?

Many people use 5–7% for a diversified stock portfolio over long periods, but returns aren't guaranteed. Try a lower number to stress-test your decision.

Is this financial advice?

No. It's a model; real outcomes depend on your market and choices.
