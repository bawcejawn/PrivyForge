Compress an image to under 20 KB

Get a signature scan, thumbnail or small photo under 20 KB at the best quality that fits. Everything happens in your browser.

Drop images here to get them under 20 KB JPG · PNG · WebP · AVIF · HEIC — any number

JPG is accepted almost everywhere; WebP is smaller, for websites. Each file is made as close to the limit as possible without going over. 1 KB is counted as 1,000 bytes, so files also pass sites that count 1 KB as 1,024 bytes.

    Each file shows its final size, quality and dimensions.

    How to compress an image to 20 KB

    1. Prepare the image. For a signature, sign in dark ink on plain white paper, photograph or scan it in good light, and crop tightly around the signature.
    2. Add it by dragging it onto the box, clicking to browse, or pasting.
    3. Check the target (20 KB) and keep JPG selected unless the site accepts WebP.
    4. Download the result. The final size, quality and pixel dimensions are listed next to the file.

    A worked example

    20 KB is a tight limit for a full photo. Our 2400×1600 test photo (368 KB) came out at 19.9 KB, saved at quality 62 and scaled to 602×401. Quality alone couldn’t get there without turning blotchy, so the photo was scaled down in steps to about a quarter of its original width, keeping the quality at 50 or above. A tightly cropped signature on white paper is much easier: large plain areas compress to almost nothing, so it usually fits at a higher quality and a larger size.

    Where 20 KB limits come up

    The most common place is the signature upload on online application forms, for example exam registrations, recruitment portals and government services, which often pair it with a separate, larger limit for the photograph. Some older systems also use very small limits for profile pictures, thumbnails and scanned stamps. Typical companion rules to look out for:

    • A minimum size as well as a maximum (for example “10–20 KB”). If your result is under the minimum, raise the target slightly or use a larger, less tightly cropped image.
    • Pixel dimensions for the signature box. Set exact sizes with the image resizer first if they are specified.
    • Format: usually JPG/JPEG.

    How the target size is reached

    File size depends on two things: how many pixels the image has, and the quality setting used to save it. This tool searches for the highest quality that fits first, because lowering quality a little is less noticeable than shrinking the picture:

    1. It tries quality 95. If that already fits, you get it.
    2. Otherwise it tries quality 50. If that fits, it narrows down between the two (a binary search) to find the highest quality that still fits, in at most eight more attempts.
    3. If even quality 50 is too big, it shrinks the dimensions step by step (to 85% each time, or more when the file is far too big) and searches again.

    The result is never over your limit. In the rare case the limit is impossible (for example 5 KB for a detailed photo), the tool says so and shows the smallest file it could make.

    What if it can’t reach 20 KB?

    For almost any photo or scan, shrinking the dimensions eventually gets the file under 20 KB. If it would have to shrink below 16 pixels on its shortest side, the tool stops and tells you clearly that the target wasn’t reached, showing the smallest file it could make, rather than quietly handing you a file that would be rejected. In that case, crop the image more tightly or ask whether the form accepts a larger file.

    Tips for a clean signature under 20 KB

    • Use dark ink on white paper. High contrast keeps the strokes crisp even at low quality.
    • Shoot in daylight without shadows. Grey, uneven paper costs bytes and looks dirty after compression.
    • Crop tightly. Empty margins add pixels the form doesn’t need.
    • Don’t fight the downscale. A signature a few hundred pixels wide is perfectly legible; the tool shrinks only as much as it must.

    For photographs with a larger allowance, use compress to 50 KB or compress to 100 KB.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why did my photo get so much smaller in pixels?

    20 KB is very little room for a full photo. When even quality 50 is over the limit, the tool shrinks the dimensions step by step until it fits. Cropping first keeps more detail where it matters.

    Can I use this for a signature?

    Yes, that is the most common use. Sign in dark ink on white paper, crop tightly, then compress here; a clean signature fits in 20 KB easily.

    Why is my file a bit under 20 KB rather than exactly 20 KB?

    Quality goes up in whole steps, and the next step up was over the limit. The tool always picks the highest quality that fits.

    Should I choose JPG or WebP?

    JPG for forms and portals, because many accept only JPG/JPEG. WebP gives smaller files for your own website.

    Are my photos uploaded?

    No. Everything happens in your browser, so ID photos and documents never leave your device. The page works offline after the first visit.
